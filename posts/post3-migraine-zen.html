Migraine Zen: An Accessible App for Tracking Headaches and Migraines

Published: November 25, 2026

No matter who you are or what stage of life you're in, I think we all have our issues. Some might be childhood trauma, others physical, and others mental or emotional. It seems to be one thing that brings us together.

One of my issues has been headaches and migraines. When I was a kid, I didn't have many of either. During college they started infrequently, and ever since they've slowly ramped up in both how often they occur and their intensity.

I'm not wanting to talk medicine. I think treatment options should be between yourself and your doctor, nurse practitioner, or other healthcare provider.

What I would like to talk about is a handy app named Migraine Zen.

In the last six months, my migraines had really ramped up. I know it's because of coming off a migraine preventative and looking for alternative prevention methods—medicine or otherwise. I also knew it would be good to track my headaches and see if triggers could be identified.

When I did research through AI and the internet, I found there are quite a few triggers as well as a lot of apps that try to help. By far, though, the vast majority were either subscription-based or free. Few were one-time purchase options. Further, I needed the app to be VoiceOver compatible so it would work with my iPhone.

Exploring the free options, most were not accessible. Among the few that were either partially or fully accessible, I found they didn't track enough, log much data, or both.

Even the apps that offered free and paid options suffered from one or both of those drawbacks.

Finally, I found Migraine Zen. It was a one-time purchase with no free version. I had to take a chance on it, but I'm glad I did.

The app was almost completely accessible, full of good information, and allowed me to log headaches, intensities, duration, relief measures, and more. It gives useful information like headache history, tracks trends, and even tries to predict when migraines might be more probable. It tracks weather conditions if you turn it on, asks about triggers from different categories you think might be possible, and gives useful feedback. It is a great little app if you use an iPhone and have an Apple Watch. Why do I mention the Apple Watch? Well, it tracks sleep states, especially something it calls the REM fragment index, and uses that with other information to try to predict migraines. I don't know what other devices it might be compatible with, like Android phones or other sleep-tracking devices.

The app is actively developed, the developer seems to be open to feedback, and it's been great for me.

In short, I encourage anyone to go through their own research process and find what's right for them if you or they need an app similar to what I've outlined. But if you want the features I've described, this app is extremely accessible. The few buttons that aren't labeled are easy to figure out, and I predict they will gain labels sometime in the near future.
