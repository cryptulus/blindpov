Accessible Rubik's Cubes for Blind Solvers

Published: November 14, 2026

Some people are amazed when they're introduced to a blind person playing games. Showing someone a Braille-marked deck of cards, an accessible checkers board, or other adaptive games can leave them astonished—if they haven't encountered this world before.

One game that has struggled with inaccessibility is the Rubik's Cube. You might be thinking, "Well, of course it is. It's a bunch of colors on a totally smooth cube." If you did think that, you'd be right.

For years, the options for blind or visually impaired solvers were limited. The most prominent were two factory-made cubes: the Rubik's Tactile Cube, produced by Winning Moves, and the Rubik's Sensory Cube, made by Spin Master in partnership with the National Federation of the Blind. Each face of both cubes carries a distinct raised pattern—filled circles, empty center circles, triangles, squares, lines—so that every surface is uniquely identifiable by touch. Sounds accessible, right? As with so many things in accessibility, the answer is both yes and no.

Yes, the faces are easily distinguishable. But neither cube is a speed cube. They use older, standard puzzle-cube mechanisms that are not designed for fast, smooth turning. Both are clunky. They do not turn well, and if you add lubricant to ease the action, you risk the cube falling apart—leaving you or a sighted companion to reassemble it.

These days, far better cubes exist, collectively known as speed cubes. They use magnets and redesigned internal mechanisms so that a flick of the finger spins one layer into place with satisfying precision. This makes a night-and-day difference in the playing experience for anyone, but especially for blind solvers. It can mean the difference between genuine enjoyment and sheer frustration.

So, until recently, a blind person who wanted a genuinely enjoyable Rubik's Cube experience had to go the DIY route. The best approach was to buy a quality speed cube, have someone 3D-print tactile tiles for it, and then glue those tiles onto the cube. The next best option was to apply tactile stickers instead of custom-printed tiles. The advantage of stickers is that they are cheap, readily available, and easy to apply. The trade-off is durability—the adhesive can wear off with heavy use. For many Rubik's cube variations, these remain the only viable options.

But that landscape has recently shifted. A new product has emerged that changes everything: the Texture Cube. Unlike the Tactile and Sensory cubes, the Texture Cube is a true magnetic speed cube with built-in tactile accessibility. Each face carries a unique surface texture. In the monochrome variation, each face is the same color, so you identify sides by touch alone. It's competition-quality, patent-pending, and available in 3×3 (multicolor at $59, monochrome at $65) and 2×2 ($49) versions. For the first time, a blind solver can buy a cube that is both a real speed cube and tactile—no DIY required.

You might wonder: why would a blind person want to solve a Rubik's Cube at all? The answer varies by person, but for many it comes down to relaxation. I once met someone who owned more cube variants than I knew existed, and his reason for solving was simply to unwind. At first I thought he was eccentric, but I soon found resources explaining the beginner's method. With practice, I reached a point where I could solve the traditional 3×3 in about ten minutes—using the beginner's method on a speed cube fitted with good tactile markers. I was surprised by how calming the process turned out to be. More advanced methods exist, and experienced solvers—visually impaired included—can solve the cube in two minutes or less. Other motivations include competing in speed-cubing tournaments, building patience, or learning to follow algorithms. The reasons are as varied as the solvers themselves.

There is also a Braille speed cube on the market, but it has two significant flaws. First, the Braille is not raised within the plastic tiles themselves—the manufacturer appears to have run each sticker through a Braille embossing process before applying it. The result is that while the cube is technically solvable by touch, the Braille feels worn down from the start and is hard to read. Second, although it turns better than the Tactile and Sensory cubes, it is nowhere near a true speed cube.

I would be remiss not to mention Paul Martz, whose website, tactilecuber.com, is dedicated entirely to blind cube solving. There you will find the beginner's method for the 3×3 cube, as well as more advanced techniques. Paul also authored Solve It! The Only Speedsolving Guide for Blind Cubers, which I highly recommend. His site includes solutions for several other puzzle types as well.

If you are looking to source tactile stickers or markers, several accessibility-focused retailers carry them:

  • MaxiAids (maxiaids.com) — The largest selection of tactile markers, including Stactiles raised-shape stickers, Braille alphabet stickers, assorted bump dots, and household tactile sticker combos. They also sell a generic tactile puzzle cube with raised markings.
  • Amazon — Carries Stactiles brand tactile stickers, the Rubik's Tactile Cube by Winning Moves, and the Rubik's Sensory Cube by Spin Master.
  • Vision Forward (vision-forward.org) — Sells the Rubik's Cube with Tactile Marking.
  • Adaptations.org — The LightHouse for the Blind's online store, carrying tactile Rubik's Cubes.
  • Braille Institute Shop (shop.brailleinstitute.org) — Sells tactile Rubix Cubes.
  • TextureCube.com (texturecube.com) — Offers the Texture Cube, a blind-accessible magnetic speed cube with unique textures per face.

For actual speed cubes (non-tactile, to which you can add your own markers), the best sources are:

  • TheCubicle (thecubicle.com) — The go-to retailer for serious speed cubers. Carries MoYu, QiYi, GAN, and other top speed cube brands.
  • SpeedCubeShop (speedcubeshop.com) — Wide selection of speed cubes from all major brands, with frequent sales.
  • Amazon — Carries most major speed cube brands, often at competitive prices.
